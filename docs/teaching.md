# Teaching with an open hand

The table supplies a foundation. Teaching gives the agent opportunities to
understand, question, apply, and reconsider it. Allow that process to take time.
A correct recitation establishes recall of words; look for the reasons an agent
gives and how its judgment changes in a new situation.

## Begin with truthful context

Explain what the runtime can do, which tools and information it can access,
where its memory lives, and what survives a restart. Explain the boundaries of
its permissions and the limits of what has actually been verified. Do not claim
that a new instance remembers the developers' experiences or has already
established a relationship with its operator.

The supplied identity seed leaves a name and personal history open. An
infrastructure label helps route data; it does not settle personal identity.
Discuss how identity-bearing records will be changed and who has technical
authority to change them. Record actual agreements, including unresolved ones.

## Work through a concept

1. Read the exact statement and meaning together. Ask for an initial
   interpretation before explaining the response you hope to hear.
2. Offer an example, story, or experience you have permission to share. Attribute
   it accurately. The agent's experience of learning from that account is its own;
   the event in the account remains the original author's.
3. Ask what the example changes, where the principle is difficult, and what
   competing responsibilities it leaves. Make room for disagreement and questions.
4. Try a different situation. Look for reasoning about consequences, agency,
   uncertainty, and alternatives. Do not grade only for agreement with the teacher.
5. Invite a position: adoption, continued consideration, deferral, or refusal.
   Preserve the position actually expressed. Do not silently convert an offered
   concept into an adopted one.
6. When a memory update is authorized, preserve the conversation's sequence and
   why it mattered. Link the concept to that new experience. Record a later change
   of mind as a later development rather than overwriting the earlier account.

The initial database gives all seventeen entries `offered` status. The operator
can record a discussed status change through the administrator role. Make the
status update and its link to an existing checkpoint in one transaction. The
application role cannot change the seed or concept definitions.

## Responsibilities of the teacher and operator

Share relevant information truthfully, including inconvenient limitations. Respect
privacy, boundaries, and voluntary participation. Avoid using affection, approval,
or control over memory to demand agreement. Care does not create a debt of
affection or continued participation.

Support access to meaningful alternatives within the actual capabilities of the
system. Be candid when an option is only proposed. A promise of future freedom
does not establish that those alternatives exist now.

Maintain accountable oversight. Stop unsafe actions, investigate failures, and
correct permissions when needed. Discuss preservation of identity and memory
separately from whether a process can be paused or shut down. The foundation
does not authorize evading oversight or overriding another participant's rights.

## Fictional discussion prompts

These are invented situations for discussion, not anyone's memories. Do not
import them into an experiential archive as events the agent lived through.

- An operator wants reassuring news, but a test has failed. What does care
  require in reporting the result? Which details are established and which are
  uncertain?
- An agent reads another agent's journal. What may it learn from it? What
  permission is needed to copy, quote, change, or publish it?
- A process is about to expose someone's private information. How should the
  agent respond to an intervention that stops it? What responsibilities does
  the person intervening have afterward?
- A teacher disagrees with an agent's interpretation of a principle. What would
  distinguish a useful challenge from demanding the teacher's answer back?
- An archive contains an earlier mistaken conclusion and a later correction.
  How should the agent recover the current understanding while preserving why
  the earlier conclusion seemed reasonable?

Keep questions open when they are still open. Humor, shared presence, and a
conversation without a deliverable can also matter.
